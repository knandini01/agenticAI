INCIDENT SUMMARY: Defect rate increase Q3 2023 — Line 3
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

INCIDENT DESCRIPTION:
In August 2023, production Line 3 experienced a defect rate increase
from a baseline of 1.9% to 6.8% over a 12-day period.

ROOT CAUSE IDENTIFIED:
A procurement substitution of Aluminum Alloy 6061 with Alloy 7075
from a new supplier (not named per policy) without machine recalibration.

TIMELINE OF EVENTS:
  Aug 3  — New material batch received from alternative supplier
  Aug 5  — Production resumed with new batch
  Aug 8  — First defect spike observed (defect rate 3.2%)
  Aug 12 — Defect rate reached 6.8%
  Aug 14 — Root cause identified: alloy mismatch (7075 vs 6061)
  Aug 15 — Machine M-L3-01 emergency maintenance performed
  Aug 16 — Original alloy 6061 sourced from primary supplier
  Sep 1  — Defect rate returned to 2.1%

TECHNICAL MECHANISM:
Alloy 7075 has approximately 80% higher tensile strength than 6061.
When processed on machines calibrated for 6061, the harder material:
  1. Generates 40-60% more cutting heat
  2. Causes spindle temperatures to exceed 80°C threshold
  3. Produces thermal micro-warping in precision cutting surfaces
  4. Results in dimensional non-conformance → defects

This mechanism was confirmed by correlation analysis and machine
diagnostic logs showing temperature increase preceding defect spike.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
  Lost production: ~$240,000
  Repair costs: $18,500
  Customer penalty: $75,000
  Total impact: ~$333,500

RECOMMENDATIONS (MANDATORY):
  1. NO alloy substitution without Quality Engineering sign-off
  2. Any 7000-series alloy requires machine recalibration and pilot run
  3. Supplier changes require 30-day qualification period
  4. Quality hold must be respected — no waivers for alloy grade mismatches
  5. Incoming material hardness testing must precede production release

STATUS: Recommendations implemented on Line 3.
NOTE: These recommendations apply to ALL production lines.
